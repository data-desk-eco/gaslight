Undeclared flaring in the Permian Basin

Are operators flaring without declaring it? Satellite flare observations — VIIRS Nightfire and Sentinel-2 — cross-matched against the flaring volumes operators declare to the Railroad Commission of Texas say yes: roughly a quarter of all sustained satellite-observed flare activity at producing leases has zero flared gas declared for the month in question.

The headline numbers

For each of 1,297 VIIRS flare sites we collected every lease with a well inside 375 m — half a VIIRS pixel — plus the per-well gas-well IDs sharing the same district, operator and lease name (the RRC well file’s gas-well keys are stale, so gas-well reporting is invisible to a purely spatial join), and summed the flaring those leases declared to the RRC for each month the satellite saw fire on three or more nights. Sites within 1 km of a gas processing plant are excluded: plant flaring is legitimate non-lease reporting, and the plant list unions the RRC’s R-3 facilities with the EIA-757 plant survey, plus an imagery review of every named case.

In 3,117 site-months a flare burned for at least three nights while every lease within the pixel reported gas production but not one MCF flared. At the median declared-flaring rate of ~705 MCF per MW of observed radiant heat, that implies very roughly ~10.8 BCF of flared gas over five years that never appears in RRC disposition records — an order of magnitude, not an estimate.

Anadarko / Oxy · Loving County

The flare that never existed

43undeclared months
458flare-nights
0MCF ever declared

Nothing has ever been declared at this site — not on the Tabasco Cat oil lease, not on its gas-well ID either. The flare burned through every year from 2021 to 2025 while the matched leases produced 0.45–1.37 BCF a year. Sentinel-2 places the flame 178 m from Anadarko’s wellheads; the nearest midstream facility in any of the R-3, EIA-757 or EPA GHGRP lists is over 5 km away. Methane plumes were imaged nearby in 2021 (158 kg/hr) and 2025 (439 kg/hr).

The sharp 2024 spike is a second story: it coincides exactly with neighbouring Admiral Permian’s SWR-32 flaring exceptions at the adjacent Hamburglar pads (June–October 2024, up to 7,500 MCF/day permitted) — flowback that Admiral was licensed to burn but never declared a single MCF of in 2024. Anadarko also tops the frequency rollup: 199 undeclared months across 40 single-operator sites — high frequency, low intensity.

XTO Energy · Martin County

Pyke & Merrick: the year the paperwork went quiet

107nights in 2024
0MCF declared in 2024
~3,400kg/hr peak plume

The gap case. XTO declared 2,063–4,330 MCF a year in 2021–23. Then in 2024 — the flare’s biggest year on record, 107 nights and 561 MW — the leases declared zero. Declarations resume in 2025, at 30,511 MCF through October.

This is not a company-wide lapse: XTO declared 708 MMCF of flaring across 478 other leases in 2024. And it is XTO’s own flare — the company filed “Merrick CTB Flare” SWR-32 exceptions 45 m from the satellite fix, on the tank battery visible in imagery. Carbon Mapper plumes up to ~3,400 kg/hr have been imaged around the Pyke and Merrick pads. Burning more than ever in the one year the paperwork goes quiet is hard to read as an oversight.

HighPeak Energy · Howard County

O’Daniel Ranch: reporting that flickers

25undeclared months
260,734MCF declared in 2023
276nights/yr at peak

The counter-example that sharpens the rest: HighPeak does declare — up to 260,734 MCF in 2023, among the basin’s biggest declarers — yet still logs 25 sustained months with zero declared while the flare burned up to 276 nights a year.

Across its three sites: 70 undeclared months against only 19 declared. And after March 2025 HighPeak’s disposition-04 filings stop basin-wide, while these leases produced another ~0.85 BCF through October and the flare burned 50 more nights. Reporting that switches on and off while the fire doesn’t.

Caveats

Proximity is not ownership: 375 m is a satellite pixel, and a well list is not a deed — though for the named cases a 750 m robustness check and single-operator pixels make misattribution unlikely. The plant exclusion is only as good as the plant lists: R-3 is badly incomplete and EIA-757 locations date to 2017, so every named case was additionally screened against satellite imagery to confirm it sits on lease infrastructure, not a plant. PDQ reports can be amended; “zero declared” means zero in the current RRC extract. The radiant-heat-to-volume conversion is indicative only.

Misfiled-not-unreported was tested directly: declarations made under per-well gas-well IDs — invisible to a spatial well join because the RRC well file’s gas-well keys are stale — are folded in via district + operator + lease-name linkage, which moved ~870 site-months into the declared column (and exonerated, for instance, most of Blackbeard Operating’s apparent gap). The compressor-station alternative was checked against the R-3 and EIA-757 facility lists, EPA GHGRP reporters and TCEQ emissions-event records: none of the named cases has any such facility within 5 km, and each named operator filed SWR-32 flare exceptions at the case site itself. Residual fire that is still misfiled under an unrelated lease id remains possible — that is still a data integrity finding, just a different headline.
